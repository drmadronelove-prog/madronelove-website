    Module 01

    Day Four

    You have had a dog for ninety-six hours. Here is what is actually happening, and what is not your fault.

    Earthwind is nine months old. He left the only home he knew, arrived somewhere with unfamiliar smells, a hallway full of strangers, and one person he has known for four days. His entire frame of reference got deleted.

    What that produces is a dog who looks unpredictable. Fine one hour, unreachable the next. That is not a character flaw and it is not a training failure. It is a nervous system with no baseline yet.

    The three-week shape

    Days 1–3

    Overwhelmed. Often quiet, sometimes eerily well-behaved. Not his real personality.

    Weeks 1–3

    He starts to relax, and the actual dog appears. Testing, barking, opinions. This looks like regression. It is progress.

    Month 2–3

    He starts to believe he lives here. Routines stick. This is when training begins to hold.

    Months 9–18

    Adolescence, separately. Trained dogs appear to forget everything. They have not.

    Worth holding onto

    Nothing you observe this week is a permanent fact about him. Judge him at week four, not week one.

    What actually matters right now

    • Sleep. Adolescent dogs need fourteen to sixteen hours. Most household chaos is an overtired dog, not an under-exercised one.
    • Predictability. The same sequence of events every day does more than any technique.
    • Food out of your hand instead of a bowl, so you become the source of good outcomes.
    • Not much else. This month is for settling, not for building a resume.

    No right answer. This is here so you can read it back in a month.

    Module 02

    Reading Earthwind

    The single most useful skill you can build. Everything else in this manual depends on it.

    Arousal is one system

    Excitement and aggression run on the same wiring. Not related systems. The same one. A dog spiking with joy has exactly as little impulse control as a dog spiking with fear, which is why the friendliest moment of the day is often the one where something goes wrong.

    Below a certain level, a dog can think, hear cues, and make choices. Above it, he cannot. That line is his threshold, and no training exists above it.

    Trigger stacking

    Stressors accumulate. Each one lands before the last has cleared, and arousal ratchets up instead of resetting. A dog who bites at 6pm was usually climbing since noon.

    Try it: stack a day on him

    Tap the things that happened. Watch where he ends up.

    Nothing yet. He is asleep on the rug and life is good.

    Settling versus shutting down

    Both look like a calm dog. They are opposites. A settling dog has come down. A shut-down dog has run out of options and gone still, and the next signal he gives will come with less warning.

    The reliable tell

    Eating. A dog who takes food, chews it, and finishes a meal is under threshold. A dog who ignores food he normally loves is over it, no matter how still he looks.

    When he is over threshold

    Do not train, correct, or reason. Cut the input. The crate with the door shut, in a quiet room, is not a punishment, it is the only intervention that works up there. He comes down, and then you have a dog you can work with again.

    Module 03

    Being Alone

    Two separate skills get tangled here. Untangling them is most of the work.

    One skill is being in the crate. The other is being without you. Earthwind already has the first. He rests in there with people home. The second is the one being built.

    Which problem you have

    What you hearWhat it isWhat it needs
    Crying that winds down within 10–20 minutesProtest. Normal, and it fades.Gradual duration building
    Escalating and never stopping, drooling, damage at doors, refusing food, self-injurySeparation anxiety. A different condition.A behavior professional, sometimes medication

    A cheap indoor camera answers this in one afternoon and is the best forty dollars in this manual.

    Building duration

    The whole game is staying under the point where he panics. Ten seconds. Then thirty. Then two minutes. If a duration produces distress you went too far, and you drop back. Progress is measured in weeks and it is never a straight line.

    • 01Leave casually and predictably. Sneaking out keeps him vigilant, which is the opposite of the goal.
    • 02Drain the departure cues. Pick up your keys, put on your shoes, then sit back down. Twenty times. They stop meaning anything.
    • 03A frozen stuffed Kong goes with him. It is a distraction and a test at once.
    • 04Come home boring. Quiet entry, no greeting through the bars, let him out once he is settled rather than while he is frantic.

    You come home and the frozen Kong is untouched. What does that tell you?

    Eating is the threshold test. An untouched Kong means he spent the whole absence above the line, and the next session should be shorter, not longer.

    Limits and logistics

    Overnight

    Fine at nine months. He can hold it seven to eight hours. Crate beside your bed, not across the room.

    Daytime

    Four to five hours is the usual ceiling. A full workday needs a walker, a lunch trip, or daycare.

    More space?

    Does not help a frightened dog. Panic is not about square footage. It helps a bored one.

    Two crates

    Fine and good. Give each a consistent job: bedroom for night, living room for departures.

    Placement

    Away from the front door, backed into a corner, out of the traffic path, near where you are. The front door is where all the hallway noise arrives, and a crate parked next to it keeps him working all day instead of sleeping.

    Module 04

    The Barking

    Mini poodles are a vocal breed. In an apartment with shared walls this is the thing most likely to become a real problem, and it is very workable.

    First, which kind

    Alert barking at outside sounds

    Hardest in an apartment, because the building generates a trigger every few minutes. This is Earthwind's.

    Demand barking at you

    Annoying, very trainable. Do not pay it, pay the quiet on either side of it.

    Excitement at the door or on leash

    Manageable. Mostly an arousal problem, see Module 02.

    Barking when alone

    The one that ends leases, because you find out from a note. See Module 03.

    Management beats training in month one

    For alert barking specifically, the environment does more than any protocol. You are not trying to drown out the building, only to raise the floor enough that individual footsteps stop registering as events.

    • White noise machine, box fan, or low talk radio near the front door. Human voices blur into human voices.
    • A draft stopper along the bottom of the door. It cuts sound and, just as importantly, scent. Half of what he alerts to is smelling someone pass.
    • Crate and bed as far from the entry as the floor plan allows. Blinds closed on any window facing a walkway.

    Then change what the sound means

    Right now a noise in the hallway means intruder. You want it to mean cheese.

    Sit near the door with treats. The instant a noise happens, before he reacts, drop a treat on the floor. Every time. You are not asking him to do anything. You are pairing the sound with food, over and over, until it predicts something good.

    Timing is the whole thing

    You have to beat the bark. Once he is going, he is too aroused to learn and you are just feeding a barking dog.

    Give him a job for the ones that get through

    Teach a mat. When a noise sets him off: acknowledge it, send him to the mat, pay him there. Two barks and then a job is a realistic target. Total silence usually is not, and chasing it will frustrate you both.

    Which of these buys you the most, fastest, in week one?

    Management first. Removing the trigger outperforms both training and exercise in the first month, and it costs about thirty dollars. Correction tends to add arousal to a dog who is already aroused.

    The neighbors

    Introducing yourself and saying you are working on it converts a future complaint into a heads-up. It is the highest-return five minutes available to you.

    Timeline: four to eight weeks for meaningful change. Some of it also resolves on its own as the building stops being novel to him.

    Module 05

    A Busy Brain

    Poodles are working dogs in a small package. Legs are not the answer. Nose and brain are.

    Physical exercise alone builds an athlete with more stamina to make trouble with. Sniffing, licking, chewing and foraging are the behaviors that actually regulate a dog downward.

    Retire the food bowl

    The highest-return change in this entire manual. Every meal becomes work.

    Scatter feeding

    Kibble tossed across a towel or snuffle mat. Ten minutes of hunting beats a half hour walk.

    Frozen Kong or Toppl

    Soaked kibble with a little plain yogurt or pumpkin, frozen solid. Twenty to forty minutes.

    The cardboard box

    Kibble wrapped in crumpled paper inside a box. Costs nothing, deeply satisfying, supervise it.

    Find it

    Hide five pieces around a room, release him with a cue. Scales up forever, tires him fastest.

    Chewing is a need

    Adolescents chew because they have to. Bully sticks, yak chews, beef trachea, supervised.

    Skip entirely

    Antlers, hooves, and hard nylon bones. Slab fractures on molars are a common and expensive vet finding, and they come from exactly these.

    Training counts

    Five minute sessions of trick work. Spin, chin rest, targeting, anything. Mental effort exhausts a poodle in a way physical effort does not, and it happens to rebuild the training you are worried about losing.

    Rotate the toys

    Keep four or five out, the rest in a bin, swap weekly. Novelty is what makes a toy interesting. A dog with twenty toys available has none.

    When he is into everything

    Check the clock before you check the toy box. A dog who has been awake and stimulated all afternoon looks manic rather than sleepy. Enforced naps in the crate, an hour or two at a time, fix more chaos than another walk.

    And when he does steal something: trade, do not chase. Chasing teaches him that stealing starts a great game. Walking to the treat jar teaches him that bringing you things pays.

    Module 06

    Keeping His Training

    Two thousand dollars of professional work came with him. It is not gone. It is addressed to someone else.

    What breaks in a handover is almost never the dog forgetting. It is a vocabulary mismatch. He knows a specific word, said a specific way, followed by a specific reward. You say something slightly different and he looks blank, and it reads as training that did not stick.

    Get the four things from his trainer

    • 01The exact cue words. "Down" and "lie down" and "place" are three different words to him.
    • 02The hand signals. Often the hand is doing the actual work and the word is decoration.
    • 03His marker. A clicker, or a word like "yes," meaning correct and food is coming. Without it, half his training has no bridge to you.
    • 04What he was paid with, and whether he was still being fed for everything or had moved to intermittent.

    A video of him working with the trainer is worth more than a written list, because you will see timing and body language you would never think to ask about. Many trainers will also do one handover session.

    Maintenance is not sessions

    It is asking for something small before things he already wants. Sit before the leash clips on. Sit before the bowl goes down. Wait before the door opens. That is a dozen repetitions a day folded into things you were doing anyway, at no extra cost in time.

    Then five minutes, twice a day, on whatever you want sharp. Short beats long with a poodle.

    Two things to expect

    Pay him generously

    For the first month, even for things he already knows. He is not being retrained. He is learning that you are a source of good outcomes.

    He will test

    Around week two or three, once he is comfortable, cues that worked on day one get ignored. That is a settling dog, not a broken one.

    Teaching him to go in the crate on his own

    Toss a treat in. He walks in. Mark and feed a second one. Let him walk back out. In and out, over and over, door never closing. Once he is going in fast and confidently, you start saying the word as he commits. The word attaches to a behavior he is already doing, rather than being used to make him do it.

    Lifting him in is not harmful. It just slowly teaches him that hands near the crate mean getting put somewhere, which is a small thing now and an annoying one in six months.

    Module 07

    The Coat

    A curly coat does not shed, which is the good news and also the entire problem.

    Human curly hair sheds about a hundred strands a day into the drain. His does not. Dead hair stays in, wraps around live hair, and felts against the skin. So the job is not detangling, it is actively removing hair that has nowhere else to go.

    The daily version

    Ten to fifteen minutes with a slicker brush and a metal comb, most days. The comb is the test. Brush it out, then run a metal comb down to the skin. If the comb does not pass through, it is matted, no matter how good it looks.

    Line brushing is the technique: part the coat and work in sections from the skin outward, rather than skimming the surface.

    Mats form first at friction points. Behind the ears, armpits, where the collar or harness sits, tail base, between the toes.

    Bathing, in order

    • 01Comb him out completely first. Water shrinks tangles into felt. A mat that was workable dry becomes a shave wet.
    • 02Non-slip surface. Most dogs who hate baths hate slipping. A lick mat suctioned to the wall helps enormously.
    • 03Dilute the shampoo in a cup of water first. A dense curly coat repels undiluted shampoo, which then sits on top and never rinses out.
    • 04Keep water out of his ears. Poodles have hair in the canal and are prone to infections.
    • 05Rinse past the point where you think you are done. Residue makes him itchy and mats the coat faster.
    • 06Squeeze and blot, never rub. Rubbing a curly coat is how you make mats.
    • 07Dry with air while brushing. Air drying alone is the main cause of matted poodles, because the curl tightens as it dries and pulls the coat into itself.
    Never human shampoo

    Wrong pH for dog skin. And if the castile soap in your cabinet is the tea tree one, keep it away from him entirely. Tea tree oil is toxic to dogs.

    The hard stretch

    Somewhere between eight and fourteen months the puppy coat sheds out underneath the adult coat. That stretch mats invisibly, right at the skin, while the surface still looks fine. Earthwind is entering it now. Get through it with daily combing and you are mostly clear.

    Money and time

    Professional groom

    Every four to six weeks. Roughly $60–$85 for a twelve pound dog in the East Bay.

    Doing it yourself

    A high velocity dryer matters more than clippers. $120–$250, and it does more for the coat than anything else you can buy.

    The realistic split

    You do brushing, bathing, drying and tidying. A groomer resets the shape two or three times a year.

    Keep him short

    An inch or less. Long doodle-style coat is the high maintenance version and the source of every matting horror story.

    Start now

    Touch his paws, ears, muzzle and tail every day. A dog who was never taught to tolerate handling becomes an adult who has to be sedated to be groomed. Also: run the dryer across the room during meals for a week before you ever point it at him.

    Module 08

    Body and Danger

    The reference module. Put the two numbers in your phone before you read anything else on this page.

    Poison control, 24 hours

    ASPCA Animal Poison Control   (888) 426-4435

    Pet Poison Helpline   (855) 764-7661

    Both are staffed by veterinary toxicologists and give your vet a case number. A consultation fee applies. Do not induce vomiting unless they or a vet tell you to, since some substances do more damage coming back up.

    Know these cold

    Xylitol / birch sugar

    The worst one. Blood sugar crash, then liver failure, at tiny doses for a twelve pound dog. In sugar-free gum, mints, protein bars, toothpaste, and some peanut butters. Read every label before it goes in a Kong.

    Grapes, raisins, currants

    Acute kidney failure. No established safe amount. For his size, a few grapes is a phone call.

    Sago palm

    Common in California landscaping. Every part is toxic and it causes liver failure.

    Wild mushrooms

    Death cap grows under oaks all over Northern California in the wet months. Anything he grabs on a walk after rain is worth a call.

    Cannabis

    Now one of the most common poisoning calls in the Bay Area. Edibles combine THC with chocolate or xylitol.

    Human pain relief

    Ibuprofen, naproxen, acetaminophen. All seriously toxic. Never give any of them.

    Also: chocolate, worse the darker it is; onion, garlic, leeks and chives, including powdered; macadamia nuts; alcohol; caffeine; raw bread dough; antifreeze; nicotine and vape pods; vitamin D supplements; expanding glues. Cooked bones are not toxic but they splinter.

    The paperwork side

    • Pet insurance, enrolled before his next vet visit. Anything a vet writes down first becomes a pre-existing condition. His parents were health tested but he is not covered by anything meaningful in writing, so insurance is what actually does the job people expect a health guarantee to do.
    • A vet you like, established early rather than during an emergency, plus the nearest 24-hour emergency hospital saved in your phone.
    • His records, including which vaccines he had and when. Some vets restart a series given outside a clinic.
    Size changes the math

    He is twelve pounds. Quantities that would be trivial for a large dog matter for him. Half a bar of dark chocolate. A handful of grapes. One piece of sugar-free gum.
